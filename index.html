AI Researcher, Kyung Hee University

I work on AI agents for heart care at Kyung Hee University, outside Seoul.

Two things have to be true before any of this gets near a patient. The model has to be small enough to run on hardware a hospital already owns, and its answers have to be traceable to something. Most of what I do sits on one side or the other of that: building knowledge graphs that give a model something solid to stand on, and compressing models until they fit.

In August I presented at the ESC Congress in Munich with cardiologists from five Korean university hospitals. We had turned the Korean heart failure registry, 7,351 patients across 47 centres, into a knowledge graph, then used it to ask what happens when guideline directed therapy gets scaled back in the year after discharge. Mortality goes up, and it stays up after you adjust for how sick the patients were to begin with.

Before Korea I lectured for three years in Lahore. Before that I did a master's at NUST on matching people across surveillance cameras.

What I am working on

Agents that can show their reasoning

A multi agent system for cardiovascular diagnosis, risk explanation and patient health literacy, funded by IITP under the Korean Ministry of Science and ICT. My part is the coordination layer. What does one agent hand to the next, where does a clinical claim stop being supportable, and how does a generated sentence get checked against a symbolic knowledge base before anyone reads it.

Knowledge graphs out of raw clinical data

Registries arrive as spreadsheets, not as graphs, so the first job is building the graph. KorHF-III came out at 7,439 nodes and 114,505 edges across eight entity types, with 131 clinical properties per patient. A clinician curated chest pain knowledge base became a second one. Both are queried through a hybrid pipeline that mixes graph traversal with dense and sparse retrieval.

Making the models small enough to deploy

Quantization, knowledge distillation and structured pruning. The goal is unglamorous and specific: one checkpoint that can be served at several memory budgets, so a hospital is not storing a separate model for every machine it wants to run on.

Energy systems, on the side

Under the ITRC programme at the DESIRe centre I built a hybrid retrieval system over NREL open energy data. Before that, the self demand response component: forecasting household and facility load with LSTMs, then scheduling battery storage against price signals.
