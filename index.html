Rao Faizan

Doctoral Researcher in Artificial Intelligence · Kyung Hee University, South Korea

I build multi-agent (agentic) AI systems for cardiovascular care. That means constructing clinical knowledge graphs straight from raw registry data, reasoning over them with hybrid retrieval, and building the context-management, safety and alignment layers that decide whether an agent system can be trusted near a patient. A parallel track shrinks clinical language models far enough to run inference inside a hospital.

Research areas

Where my work sits, and the through-line connecting it: making capable models small enough, grounded enough, and trustworthy enough to deploy.

Agentic systems for healthcare

Multi-agent systems for cardiovascular diagnosis and patient health literacy: task decomposition, tool and MCP interfaces, and coordination between reasoning and retrieval agents.

Clinical knowledge graphs

Building knowledge graphs directly from raw registry data, then querying them with HybridRAG. The KorHF-III heart-failure registry became a 7,439-node, 114,505-edge graph.

Context, safety and alignment

Inter-agent context management, scope limits on clinical claims, and a proof reasoner that verifies generated statements against a symbolic knowledge base before a user sees them.

Shrinking models for deployment

Quantization, knowledge distillation and structured pruning so clinical models fit hospital-side inference hardware without losing diagnostic accuracy.

Vision–language modeling

Clinical question answering and automated report generation over chest X-ray corpora such as MIMIC.

Intelligent energy systems

HybridRAG over open energy datasets, plus LSTM demand forecasting and battery-storage scheduling for self demand response.

Currently

  • Multi-agent cardiovascular diagnostics. Leading the agent workstream of Explainable Logical Reasoning for Medical Knowledge Generation, funded by IITP under the Korean Ministry of Science and ICT.
  • Knowledge graphs from raw clinical data. KorHF-III heart-failure registry and a clinician-curated chest-pain knowledge base, served through HybridRAG over a graph database with an MCP tool interface.
  • Context management, safety and alignment. The layer that keeps a multi-agent clinical system inside its evidence. This is the part that decides whether any of it is deployable.
  • Shrinking models for hospital inference. Including BANQ, a budget-aware nested quantization scheme that serves one low-bit checkpoint across several memory budgets.
  • Data-driven energy operations. HybridRAG over NREL open energy datasets and the Self Demand Response component, under the MSIT ITRC programme (DESIRe Center).
