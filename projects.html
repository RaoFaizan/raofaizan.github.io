Explainable Logical Reasoning for Medical Knowledge Generation

2024 – Present

Kyung Hee University · IITP, Korean Ministry of Science and ICT · project IITP-2022-0-00078

  • Problem. A language model capable enough to support cardiovascular diagnosis is too large to deploy affordably, and its output cannot be taken on trust anywhere near a patient.
  • Approach. A multi-agent system whose reasoning is grounded in knowledge graphs built from raw clinical data: the KorHF-III heart-failure registry (7,351 patients, 47 centres) as 7,439 nodes and 114,505 edges, plus a clinician-curated chest-pain knowledge base. These are served through HybridRAG over a graph database behind an MCP tool interface, with a proof reasoner verifying claims against a symbolic knowledge base.
  • My role. Lead the multi-agent workstream. Own knowledge-graph construction, the context-management / safety / alignment layer, the model-efficiency work (quantization, distillation, pruning, including BANQ), and the vision–language pipeline over MIMIC-CXR.
  • Outcome. The registry analysis was accepted as an abstract at ESC Congress 2026, with cardiology departments at five Korean university hospitals.

Hybrid AI-Based Multi-Agent System for Data-Driven Energy Operations

2024 – Present

Kyung Hee University · MSIT ITRC programme · DESIRe Center (Data-driven Energy System Innovation Research Center)

  • Problem. Energy operators hold grid, building-load and market data in incompatible formats, and need demand to flex against supply and price signals without users having to think about it.
  • Approach. A HybridRAG system over NREL open energy datasets so operational questions can be answered across heterogeneous sources, alongside an automated demand response platform.
  • My role. Built the HybridRAG component, and the Self Demand Response (SDR) component before it: dataset unification across heterogeneous consumption sources, the LSTM forecasting models driving the SDR policy, and the battery-storage scheduling algorithm.

IAB-Net — Informative & Attention-Based Person Re-Identification

2020 – 2021

M.S. thesis · Machine Vision & Intelligent Systems Lab, NUST

  • Problem. Matching the same person across non-overlapping surveillance cameras breaks down under viewpoint, illumination and background variation.
  • Approach. An attention module that suppresses background clutter and emphasises discriminative body regions, improving matching robustness across cameras.
  • Outcome. Published and presented at ICoDT2 2021 (IEEE). Owned the full cycle: dataset curation, model design, ablation studies and write-up.
